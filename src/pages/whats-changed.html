Redesign preview · For H&H Builders

What changed, and why.

Every change in this preview traces back to a finding in the Digital Marketing & Content Audit. Here’s the map, plus what we need from you before launch.

Visual design & experience

01

Full-width layout for key visuals

The hero, page headers and image bands now use the full browser width. Text stays in comfortable reading columns.

See: Home hero, the Denver skyline band, every service page header.

02

A lighter, more refined palette

Warm paper and stone backgrounds replace the heavy dark palette. H&H green, taken directly from the logo, is the one strong accent. Dark green appears only in the consultation band. All text meets WCAG AA contrast.

03

No sliders: static galleries

Every carousel is gone. Projects sit in a static grid with headings and descriptions outside the images, and there are no keyboard traps.

See: Projects.

04

Before/after on hover

Project images swap to the “before” on hover. Keyboard and touch users get a real Show before button, so it isn’t a mouse-only feature.

See: Home case studies, Whole-house.

Homepage content

05

Announcement bar

A slim bar for timely news. It currently promotes the Clay Street case study.

06

A clear hero message

“Remodeling Homes… Improving Lives” becomes a headline that says what H&H does and where (Denver design-build remodeling), with a supporting line about the engineering advantage.

07

Core services up front

Six service cards, each with a one-line pitch and a link to its own page.

08

Proof near the top

A credentials strip sits right under the hero: since 1998, licensed PE, C.A.R.E. awards, Guildmaster, Big 50. Reviews follow the projects.

09

Three detailed projects

Before/after, location, scope and timeline, each linking to a full case study.

10

Easier lead capture

A consultation form at the bottom of every page, a contact page with a clear “what happens next,” and a phone number in the header. Form fields include project type and budget range to pre-qualify leads.

11

“What, Where, Why” replaced with an FAQ

A scannable FAQ that links to H&H pages instead of external sites. A full FAQ page includes FAQ structured data for Google and AI search tools.

Content & positioning

12

Owning the structural niche

“Engineer-owned” is the main message throughout, told in an engineer’s plain voice: “Yes, that wall can probably come out.”

13

Design-Build Guide

A single, shareable guide: the problem it solves, a side-by-side comparison, phases and fees, cost ranges, and 10 questions to ask. Read it.

14

Case studies with budget, timeline & surprises

Three projects rewritten in that format. The Wash Park story openly covers the mid-project foundation issue.

15

Specific about money and time

Published cost ranges by project type and a minimum project size help pre-qualify leads.

16

Team, credentials & reviews

Team bios, awards, a client portal mention, review links in the footer and on the contact page, and placeholders for Instagram, LinkedIn and YouTube.

Technical & accessibility

17

Accessibility fixes from the scan

One H1 per page, a working skip link to the main content, descriptive link text, visible focus styles, reduced-motion support, and labelled form fields.

18

Mobile speed

No page builder or slider scripts. Images are compressed WebP with responsive sizes and lazy loading. There’s one small stylesheet and one tiny script.

19

Structured data

LocalBusiness (GeneralContractor) schema with address, hours and service area, plus FAQ schema.

What we need from H&H before launch

Everything marked Placeholder or Confirm in the preview:

  • Real project photos to replace the stock images. None of the current photos show H&H work.
  • Budget ranges for the three case studies, and confirmation of the published cost tables
  • Phase 1 fee range and minimum project size
  • Three real client reviews (GuildQuality or Google) to replace the sample quotes
  • John’s approval of the draft quote on the Structural page
  • Team headshots, the client portal screenshot and its name
  • A high-resolution or vector logo file (the preview uses a traced version)
  • A Google review link, and social profile URLs

Next step: WordPress

Once you approve the look, this design becomes a native WordPress block theme on your existing site. You’ll edit pages in the standard WordPress editor, with no page-builder licence. Every section in this preview becomes a reusable block pattern, and old URLs get redirects so search rankings carry over.
