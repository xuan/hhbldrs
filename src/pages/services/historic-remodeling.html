Historic home remodeling · Denver metro

Keep the character. Lose the cramped rooms.

Denver’s pre-1930 homes are beautiful and hard to remodel. Many have load-bearing brick walls, and some sit in designated historic districts with design review. We’ve worked through both, many times.

Talk about your older home

Is my home “historic” for permitting?

For permitting purposes, there are three situations in the Denver area:

What review your project needs
Your homeWhat it means
Historic home in a designated districtExterior changes need Landmark Preservation review, plus a building permit.
Newer home in a designated districtSame exterior review applies, even though the house isn’t old.
Historic home not in a districtThe most common case. A standard building permit only.

Not sure which you are? We check during the first visit. Design review adds time, typically several weeks Confirm, so we build it into your schedule from the start.

Old brick needs an engineer

Early Denver had more clay than lumber, so many older homes have load-bearing brick walls. Brick that’s cut, opened or modified without proper shoring and reinforcement can fail. Our owner is a structural engineer, so openings in brick are designed and detailed before a saw touches them.

Update it, or restore it

Some clients want modern living inside a historic shell: an open kitchen, a primary suite, a second story set back from the street. Others want the house returned to how it looked in 1910. We do both, and we keep what’s worth keeping: original floors, millwork, porches and ceilings.
