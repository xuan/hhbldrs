Additions & pop-tops · Denver metro

Outgrown the house, but not the neighborhood?

A second story (a pop-top) or a well-placed addition gets you the space without the move. Adding to a house means adding load, so the foundation, framing and zoning limits get worked out before anyone draws the pretty pictures.

What we add

We’ve built nearly every kind: second stories, primary suites, family rooms, kitchen bump-outs, attic conversions, three-story additions, balconies, decks and garages. They all share one requirement: the existing house has to carry new loads, and the new space has to look like it was always there.

A Denver pop-top, step by step

  1. Feasibility (weeks 1–3)Zoning check, including bulk plane, height and setbacks. Then a foundation and framing assessment, and a first budget range.
  2. Design & engineering (months 1–4)Floor plans, elevations, structural drawings, and selections that fit the budget.
  3. Permitting (6–12 weeks)City of Denver review. Longer in designated historic districts. Confirm current timing
  4. Construction (5–9 months)Roof off, new structure, weather-tight as fast as possible, then interiors. Most families move out for this phase.

Bulk plane, in plain words: Denver zoning draws an invisible slanted “tent” over your lot, starting at a set height on the side lot lines. Your new second story has to fit inside it. On narrow lots that shapes the rooflines, ceiling heights and where dormers can go. We design to it from the start. On Humboldt Street we worked with the city to fit a vaulted ceiling inside it.

Should we add on, or remodel what we have?

Honestly, start inside. Reworking a poor layout is often cheaper per usable square foot than adding new ones. We’ll show you both options in Phase 1 so you can compare.
