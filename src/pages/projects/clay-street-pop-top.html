Case study · Clay Street, Denver

They asked for one bathroom. They got a whole new house.

Brandon and Carlos wanted a bathroom for their daughter Nora, an office and a bigger kitchen. Design-build showed them a better option: go up.

The wish list

  • A bathroom for Nora
  • A home office
  • A larger kitchen
  • A house that worked better overall

Like most Denver homeowners, they expected trade-offs. The laundry sat inside the kitchen, there was no real primary suite, and every option for adding a room ate into something else.

What we saw on the first visit

Adding rooms piecemeal would have meant five compromises. Instead we looked at the structure as a whole and proposed a pop-top second story. It created space where the lot had none, which freed up the main floor for everything else.

  • New upstairs primary suite
  • The old primary bedroom becomes Nora’s room, with a bathroom
  • Nora’s old room becomes the office
  • The kitchen–laundry wall comes out, the laundry moves, and the kitchen grows

The structural work

The best kitchen remodels usually change the structure, not just the cabinets. Here we removed the wall between the kitchen and laundry, plus a half wall dividing the living spaces. We carried those loads on new beams sized during design, and reinforced the existing structure to support the new second story.

Living through it

Because the roof came off, the family stayed with relatives during construction. For major structural work that usually shortens the schedule and lowers stress. They stayed closely involved and watched the house change over eight months.

The result

Every item on the original list, plus several they hadn’t thought possible: a primary suite, a room and bath for Nora, a real office, a much larger kitchen, a laundry room, and a floor plan that flows.
