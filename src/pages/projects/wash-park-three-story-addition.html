Case study · Logan Street, Washington Park West

A 1901 corner house, rebuilt from the foundation up.

An attorney working from home, a physician, and three kids had outgrown a home they loved. They chose a three-story addition and a whole-house remodel over moving.

The challenge

  • The home office opened directly off the dining room, so focused work was impossible
  • No central place for the family to gather
  • Too few bedrooms for five people, plus an au pair
  • A closed-off, undersized galley kitchen

Then there was the house itself: built in 1901, on a highly visible corner, with a structure that wouldn’t allow simple layout changes.

The solution

A three-story vertical addition with space on the basement, main and second floors, combined with a remodel of every level:

  1. Kitchen as family hub: opened into the dining room, with a large island for meals, homework and everything in between.
  2. Two new children’s bedrooms upstairs and a basement suite for the au pair.
  3. The old kitchen became a mudroom, and the office was separated from family space.
  4. Historic character kept: details like a curved wall near the sink blend old and new.
What surprised us

Midway through construction we found a foundation problem that wasn’t visible before demolition. We stabilized it right away, paused that part of the work, and engineered a permanent fix. Homes built before modern foundation standards often hide issues like this. Because our engineer is on the team, the fix was designed in-house, with no outside consultant to bring up to speed.

Building around a working family

The family lived in the house for most of the project, and one parent worked from home full-time. Careful sequencing and constant communication kept the house livable and the office quiet enough to work in.

Why it took 14 months

The foundation repair, the scale of a three-story addition, and working around an occupied home all added time. We said so up front, and again when the foundation issue came up.

The result

A real gathering space, a quiet place to work, enough bedrooms for everyone, and a 1901 house that still looks like itself from the street. Above all, they stayed in the neighborhood they loved.
