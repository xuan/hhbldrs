Case study · Humboldt Street, Denver

A second story for a 1921 bungalow, and a baby born mid-build.

Two working professionals, a four-year-old, an elderly golden retriever, and a newborn on the way. We took the roof off a century-old house and kept the family living in it.

The goals

  1. Add real space to a tiny single-story 1921 home
  2. Remodel almost all of the existing ground floor

Working within bulk plane

The neighborhood’s zoning tightly limits how much vertical space a house can use (Denver calls this the bulk plane). We worked closely with the city to get the most square footage it allowed, and still fit in a vaulted ceiling on the new second floor.

Upstairs: all new

  • A primary suite with a steam shower (a mid-project request we made room for)
  • Two children’s bedrooms, one a nursery for the baby born during construction
  • Skylights in the hall and primary bath, also added mid-project
  • A large front balcony with a child-safe stainless cable railing. We worked with the city during construction to extend it as far as allowed.
  • A solar-ready roof, before Denver required one

Downstairs: opened up, character kept

  • Removed a structural wall by the front door, doubling the living room
  • Turned the old primary bedroom into a sitting room
  • Added the new stair
  • Preserved the original ceiling, millwork, front porch and hardwood floors
  • Re-sided the whole house so the addition looks original
Living through it

The family kept the finished basement and kitchen throughout. With a newborn and an old dog in the house, we kept the site free of dust and stray nails every day. Most clients can stay home during a remodel like this, though it does go faster if you can live elsewhere.
